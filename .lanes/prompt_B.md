## Experiment 1: is there a contractor effect on slip, given the contract? (existing definitive-contract panel)
1. Fit the contract-shape gradient boosting model for schedule_slip_gt90 and schedule_slip_gt365 at 24 and 36 months with NO contractor identity and NO history features (the `gbm_no_history` feature set minus anything keyed to the recipient). Produce out-of-sample predicted probabilities for validation and test rows.
2. Contractor residual persistence: for each recipient_uei with at least 5 awards in the training years and at least 3 in the test years, compute the mean residual (realised minus predicted) in train and in test. Report the correlation across contractors, weighted and unweighted, with a bootstrap interval, and the AUC of "train-period mean residual" as a predictor of slip on that contractor's test awards. Do the same for awarding_office_code so the two effects are side by side.
3. Variance decomposition: on the test rows, regress the residual on contractor fixed effects and separately on office fixed effects (or equivalently compute the between-group share of residual variance with a shrinkage/random-effects estimate); report the share of residual variance attributable to contractor vs office vs neither.
4. Within-office contrasts: among test awards in the same awarding_office_code, same psc1, same base FY and within one log-decile of size, pair awards from different contractors; ask whether the contractor with the lower training-period residual slips less often. Report the pairwise accuracy with n and a permutation null (shuffle contractor labels within office).
5. Placebo: repeat 2 with contractor identities permuted within office.
Answer in one sentence at the top of the report: what fraction of the slip signal is the contractor.

## Experiment 2: holders of the same multiple-award vehicle as a competition set (new data: delivery orders)
Orders under an indefinite-delivery vehicle (award_type_code C) are competed among the vehicle's holders, so the holders are the observable set of competing bidders, and every holder's realised slip on its own orders is public.
1. Fetch delivery orders (prime_award_types ["C"]) FY2010 to FY2026 with the same bulk-download fetcher and column list as `usaspending/src/bulk_fetch.py` (do not modify it; call it with the type parameter if it has one, or copy it into `bidders_us/src/` with the one-line change). Size it first with `POST /api/v2/search/spending_by_award_count/` per FY; delivery orders are several times more numerous than definitive contracts. Keep to the 6 GB budget: if the per-FY parquet would exceed it, restrict to orders with base federal_action_obligation >= 250,000 at conversion time and say so. Parent linkage is `parent_award_id_piid` plus the parent's agency identifier (check which column carries it; `referenced_idv_agency_iden` is the FPDS name).
2. Build order-level base rows and slip labels at 12, 24, 36 months exactly as the definitive-contract panel does (reuse the per-action logic). Competition set = distinct recipient_uei with at least one order under the same parent vehicle in the training window. Keep vehicles with at least 3 holders and at least 30 orders in the training years and at least 10 orders in the test years.
3. Forward-chained holder forecasts: for orders in the test years, forecast slip with (a) contract-shape features only (order size, planned duration, PSC, NAICS, office, pricing, competition fields), (b) (a) plus the holder's prior slip rate under this vehicle, (c) (a) plus the holder's prior slip rate on all its orders and contracts, (d) (a) plus vehicle fixed effect only. Evaluate within-vehicle AUC (pairs of test orders under the same vehicle from different holders), overall AUC, Brier skill vs the vehicle base rate, and the within-vehicle Spearman correlation between the holder's training-period slip rate and its test-period slip rate (weighted by orders). Placebo: shuffle holder identity within vehicle.
4. Report the distribution across vehicles of the gap between the best and worst holder's realised test slip rate, so a reader sees how much choosing the holder could matter if it were predictable, next to how much of that gap the history forecast recovers.
